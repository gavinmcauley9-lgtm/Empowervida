Lab #1

FASTING INSULIN

Why Your Doctor Misses It:

Standard tests only check blood glucose. But insulin resistance begins 10-15 years BEFORE blood sugar rises.

What It Reveals:

How hard your pancreas is working to control blood sugar. High insulin = early metabolic dysfunction.

Optimal Range:

< 5 µIU/mL (fasting)

What To Do If High:

Reduce refined carbs, increase protein, practice time-restricted eating (16:8).

Lab #2

HbA1c (WITH CONTEXT)

Why Your Doctor Misses It:

They test it, but accept "normal" ranges that are too high. "Normal" ≠ Optimal.

What It Reveals:

Your 3-month average blood sugar. Even "pre-diabetic" levels accelerate aging.

Optimal Range:

< 5.0% (not the standard < 5.7%)

What To Do If High:

Low-glycemic diet, resistance training 3x/week, supplement with berberine or metformin (consult your physician).

Lab #3

ADVANCED LIPID PANEL (ApoB, LDL-P)

Why Your Doctor Misses It:

Standard cholesterol tests miss 50% of people at risk. Total cholesterol and LDL-C are outdated markers.

What It Reveals:

ApoB = actual number of cholesterol-carrying particles. This is THE marker for cardiovascular risk.

Optimal Range:

ApoB < 80 mg/dL

LDL-P < 1000 nmol/L

What To Do If High:

Omega-3s (2-3g/day), reduce saturated fat, increase fiber, consider statin if ApoB > 100.

Lab #4

hs-CRP (HIGH-SENSITIVITY C-REACTIVE PROTEIN)

Why Your Doctor Misses It:

Standard CRP only catches acute inflammation. hs-CRP reveals chronic, silent inflammation—the root of most disease.

What It Reveals:

Systemic inflammation driving heart disease, diabetes, Alzheimer's, and cancer.

Optimal Range:

< 1.0 mg/L (ideally < 0.5 mg/L)

What To Do If High:

Eliminate processed foods, omega-3 supplementation, curcumin, improve sleep, manage stress.

Lab #5

VITAMIN D (25-OH)

Why Your Doctor Misses It:

They test it, but accept levels that are "sufficient" for bone health—not optimal for immunity, mood, and longevity.

What It Reveals:

Immune function, mood regulation, bone health, cardiovascular protection.

Optimal Range:

50-80 ng/mL (not 30-50 ng/mL)

What To Do If Low:

Supplement 4,000-6,000 IU daily (with K2), get morning sunlight, recheck in 3 months.

Bonus

HOMOCYSTEINE

Why Your Doctor Misses It:

Rarely tested unless you have a specific genetic condition.

What It Reveals:

Methylation status, cardiovascular risk, and brain health. High homocysteine = accelerated aging.

Optimal Range:

< 7 µmol/L

What To Do If High:

B-complex (methylated forms), betaine (TMG), folate, B12.
