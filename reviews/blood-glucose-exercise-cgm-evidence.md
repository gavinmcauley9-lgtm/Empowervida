# Blog 4 — Additional evidence audit: exercise and CGM (29 October 2026)

**Review branch only. Not published.**

## Post-meal walking: balanced interpretation

- Engeroff T, Groneberg DA, Wilke J. *Sports Medicine* 2023;53:849–869. PMID **36715875**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36715875/ — Meta-analysis of 8 crossover RCTs (116 participants); post-meal exercise reduced **acute** glucose excursions compared with pre-meal exercise and no exercise. Trials were rated at high risk of bias. **Do not extrapolate to reduced cardiovascular events.**
- Slebe R et al. *Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice* 2024;210:111638. PMID **38548105**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548105/ — Larger meta-analysis (28 intervention studies) found no clear advantage of pre- versus post-meal activity for glycaemia overall, particularly longer-term outcomes; evidence low-to-moderate certainty and heterogeneous. **Explicitly acknowledge mixed findings on timing.**
- Brian MS et al. *Journal of Sports Sciences* 2024. PMID **38858835**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38858835/ — Randomised crossover study: post-meal walking improved postprandial glucose but not 24-hour glucose control. Supports modest practical advice, not a universal 10-minute prescription.

**Recommended copy:** “A short walk after eating is a practical way to add movement to the day and may reduce the immediate rise in glucose. Research on whether exercising before or after meals is consistently better is mixed, and a smaller glucose peak has not itself been shown to prevent cardiovascular events in otherwise healthy people.”

## CGM in people without diabetes

- Johansson M, Dower JA, Lipska KJ. *JAMA Internal Medicine* 2026;186(9):1182. DOI **10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.2769**. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2851942 — Clinical patient-facing evidence summary: no good evidence CGM improves health or prevents diabetes in people without diabetes; overinterpretation may promote unnecessary food restrictions.
- *Continuous Glucose Monitoring in Type 2 Diabetes and Beyond: A Review.* *JAMA Internal Medicine* 2026;186(9):1166–1173. DOI **10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.2772**. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/article-abstract/2851941 — Reviews randomised trials and guidelines; in type 2 diabetes modest mean HbA1c benefit (~0.3%), while evidence for prediabetes and obesity without glucose-lowering therapy is limited and indirect.
- *Continuous glucose monitoring in non-diabetic populations: a systematic review of observational and interventional studies with meta-analysis.* PMID **41588451**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41588451/ — 23 studies, 1,074 people, short-term average glucose improvements in some populations, no significant BMI improvement; no appreciable glycaemic benefit in healthy normoglycaemic participants. **Nuance:** some surrogate benefits do not establish prevention of disease or longer-term outcomes.

**Recommended copy:** “CGMs can help selected patients manage diabetes. For people without diabetes, small studies suggest they can change short-term behaviour or glucose readings, but we do not yet know that routine use prevents diabetes, heart disease or other important outcomes.”

## Editorial decision

The evidence supports encouraging regular activity and appropriate diagnostic testing, **not** alarm about every glucose excursion or universal CGM use. Integrate both supporting and conflicting exercise evidence. Do not use “post-meal walking prevents cardiovascular disease” as a claim.
