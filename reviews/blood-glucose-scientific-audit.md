# Blog 4 — Scientific reference audit (29 October 2026)

**Status:** Preliminary verified-source audit, not final sign-off. Draft remains separate from the live article. No merge or deploy authorised.

## Verified authoritative sources

1. **ADA Professional Practice Committee, Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026.** Diabetes Care 49(Suppl 1):S27–S49. DOI: 10.2337/dc26-S002. https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926
   - Confirms diabetes thresholds in nonpregnant individuals: fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, 2-hour 75g OGTT ≥11.1 mmol/L, HbA1c ≥6.5% (48 mmol/mol); confirmation needed without unequivocal hyperglycaemia.
   - Fasting glucose, OGTT and HbA1c identify incompletely overlapping populations; OGTT can detect more people with impaired glucose tolerance.
   - Explicitly states evidence is insufficient to use CGM for screening or diagnosis of prediabetes or diabetes.
   - Supports screening based on risk, not blanket CGM use.
2. **Australian Diabetes Society, HbA1c for Diagnosis of Diabetes Mellitus, May 2023.** https://www.diabetessociety.com.au/guideline/hba1c-for-diagnosis-of-diabetes-mellitus-may-2023/
   - Confirms Australian diagnostic criteria: HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%), fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, OGTT two-hour ≥11.1 mmol/L. Repeat testing for confirmation in asymptomatic people.
3. **Healthdirect Australia, HbA1c test.** https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/procedures/diagnostic-tests-and-scans/blood-tests/hba1c-test
   - Supports explanation that HbA1c reflects average glycaemia over approximately 8–12 weeks and may be affected by conditions such as anaemia, iron deficiency, kidney failure and pregnancy.

## Findings requiring edits or qualification before integration

- **Australian context:** State that criteria cited apply to nonpregnant adults; pregnancy uses distinct pathways. Do not transplant all US prediabetes terminology/thresholds uncritically into Australian practice.
- **CGM:** Keep distinction between demonstrated benefits in selected diabetes care and uncertain clinical benefit for routine use in people without diabetes.
- **Post-meal glucose:** No physical glucose-crystal endothelial abrasion. Avoid claims that every rise causes arterial injury; discuss persistent dysglycaemia and multifactorial CVD risk.
- **Insulin resistance:** No universal diagnostic HOMA-IR threshold of 1.5. No absolute claim that insulin prevents weight loss.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Fatigue and brain fog alone cannot establish reactive hypoglycaemia.
- **Berberine/vinegar:** Remove treatment equivalence to metformin, universal dose recommendations and unsupported numerical effect claims. Any discussion should explicitly note evidence limitations and safety.
- **Movement:** General advice is supportable; precise effects and optimal post-meal duration still need a high-quality review/RCT citation.
- **Prevention:** Add an authoritative trial or guideline on lifestyle interventions and long-term outcomes rather than relying only on acute glucose-trace changes.
- **Narrative:** Do not invent personal clinical anecdotes attributed to the author.
