# Taurine & longevity — scientific editorial review (4 November 2026)

Status: review only; do not publish until integrated, built, and checked.

## Editorial recommendation
Retain the 2023 mouse lifespan finding, 2025 challenges to circulating taurine as an age biomarker, and add the newly published 2026 mechanistic findings. Avoid claims of proven human lifespan benefit.

## Verified primary sources
- Singh et al., Science (2023), PMID 37289866: supplementation extended lifespan in mice and improved selected health measures in animal models; not proof in humans.
- Fernandez et al., Science (2025), PMID 40472098: circulating taurine often stable or increasing with age in studied cohorts; not a reliable universal ageing biomarker.
- Experimental Evidence Against Taurine Deficiency as a Driver of Aging in Humans, Aging Cell (2025), PMID 41061678: cross-sectional study of 137 men found no association of circulating taurine with age or measured performance; limited generalisability.
- Taurine lowers inflammaging by regulating the NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages, Cell (2026), PMID 42914977, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.061: mechanistic macrophage work, aged-mouse healthspan and inflammatory outcomes, and human adipose observations under caloric restriction. This does not establish human longevity benefit from supplementation.

## Suggested insertion after 'Why researchers remain interested'
<h2>A new clue in 2026: taurine and inflammation</h2>
<p>A 2026 study in <em>Cell</em> adds an intriguing twist. Researchers investigated how taurine interacts with the NLRP3 inflammasome—a molecular alarm system in immune cells that can amplify inflammation. In laboratory and animal experiments, taurine helped restrain this pathway, and raising taurine in older mice improved selected health measures. The investigators also observed higher taurine in fat tissue alongside lower inflammation in humans undergoing sustained calorie restriction.</p>
<p>These findings offer a possible explanation for some of taurine's effects without requiring the idea that everyone's blood taurine falls with age. But the human observations do not show that taking a taurine supplement produces the same result, and the study did not demonstrate longer human life. It is a promising mechanism, not a clinical prescription.</p>
